# Build a Wonging Tracker, Then Measure Its Edge

You can build this tool with off-the-shelf parts, but it only makes sense as a measurement instrument at first, not as a money-maker. **Configure it as an 8-deck game where the dealer stands on soft 17, blackjack pays 3:2, there is no double after split and no resplit, and the published house edge is about 0.71% (RTP 99.28–99.29%).** **Count with Hi-Lo, working out decks remaining exactly from the cards seen.** **Alert only when the true count reaches about +3 and a seat is actually free.** The vision side has a proven recipe: a small YOLO model fine-tuned on 100–200 screenshots of your actual table reached 99.5% mAP@50 in one write-up. To avoid double-counting, assign each card to a fixed seat-and-position slot. Claude vision handles uncertain crops in the background and is not in the main loop. Three things are unconfirmed and you will have to settle them by watching: **penetration** (reported as anywhere from ~50% to ~75%, never measured), **whether the dealer peeks with a 10 up**, and **how a 7-seat table lets a spectator take a seat mid-shoe and keep it**. All three decide whether there is any edge at all. At ~50% penetration on 8 decks, published data and practitioners agree that playable counts are rare and arrive late in the shoe. Legally, the binding risk is the casino's terms of service. Bot and third-party-software clauses, plus "irregular play" clauses, almost certainly cover a screen-reading counter. The likely outcome if caught is that winnings are voided and the account is closed. Prosecution is unlikely but cannot be ruled out, particularly in the UK. The report ends with a checklist of what to log so that your own observations, not guesses, fill those gaps.

## Configure 8D S17 no-DAS, but treat peek and penetration as unknowns

Every source that names a deck count puts Evolution's classic 7-seat tables at **8 decks, dealer stands on all 17s, blackjack 3:2**. Evolution's own operator rule text confirms 8 decks ([theScore Bet help: Salon Privé](https://thescorebethelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/13873608581645-Salon-Prive-Blackjack-A)). The other rules come from secondary sources. You may double on any two cards, but **there is no double after split** ("Pragmatic's tables allow you to double your bet after splitting a pair. Evolution's don't" ([BTCGOSU](https://www.btcgosu.com/blog/blackjack-pragmatic-evolution/))). Pairs split only once, and split aces get one card each. There is no surrender. Insurance pays 2:1, costs half the bet, and even money is offered ([Wizard of Vegas, 2021](https://wizardofvegas.com/forum/gambling/blackjack/36643-lightning-blackjack-from-evolution/); [theScore: VIP Alpha](https://thescorebethelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/13873609104397-Blackjack-VIP-Alpha)). One affiliate claims doubling is limited to 9–11 ([Online Casino Directory](https://online.worldcasinodirectory.com/evolution-gaming/blackjack-live)). Treat that as a toggle until you check it. Rules also drift over time. Pragmatic quietly added double after split in June 2025, which raised its RTP from 99.29% to 99.41% ([BlackjackInfo](https://www.blackjackinfo.com/live-blackjack-game/pragmatic-play/blackjack-2/)). So the tool should store a rules profile per table and date it. Do not hard-code one global rule set.

The **dealer hole card** is the most consequential disputed rule. Everyone agrees the dealer peeks when an Ace is showing. One 2021 forum post about Evolution says the dealer "does NOT peek with 10 showing" ([Wizard of Vegas](https://wizardofvegas.com/forum/gambling/blackjack/36643-lightning-blackjack-from-evolution/)). An October 2026 affiliate page says Evolution plays "Vegas rules", but its wording only confirms the peek on an Ace ([Live Casino Comparer](https://www.livecasinocomparer.com/casino-guides/blackjack-table-rules/)). If there is no peek on a 10, you also need to know whether doubled and split money is lost to a dealer blackjack (full no-hole-card rules) or only the original bet. No-hole-card play costs about **0.11%** of edge, and "original bets only" cancels that penalty ([Wikipedia: Blackjack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackjack)). It also changes how you should play 11 and 10 against a dealer 10 or Ace. Model peek mode as a three-way setting: peek on Ace only, peek on Ace and 10, or no peek with original bets only.

**Penetration is the least known and the most important input.** Several affiliates repeat "roughly 50–55%" ([BTCGOSU](https://www.btcgosu.com/blog/blackjack-pragmatic-evolution/)). The same site that says the shoe is reshuffled "when half the shoe has been used" also says Evolution replaces the shoe when "a 1/4 of the shoe remains", which would be about 75% ([Live Casino Comparer: Evolution](https://www.livecasinocomparer.com/live-casino-software/evolution-live-casino-software/evolution-live-blackjack/)). No source actually measured it. A September 2026 review warns that the stream may hide the cut card's depth and that "counting rounds is not the same as counting cards" ([Blackjack Review](https://www.blackjackreview.com/wp/2026/09/09/deck-penetration-in-live-dealer-games/)). No source documents the new-shoe UI message, the two-shoe alternating layout you have seen, or any seat-timeout rule. Some Evolution tables, such as VIP Alpha, reshuffle every round ([theScore: VIP Alpha](https://thescorebethelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/13873609104397-Blackjack-VIP-Alpha)), and those must be excluded automatically. Because you will not use Bet Behind, **a free seat is a hard requirement for every alert**. Evolution's classic tables are seven seats, with Bet Behind offered precisely because the seats are often full ([Studio Spins](https://studiospins.com/blog/how-to-play-live-blackjack-online-evolution-table-families/)).

Avoid the variant tables. Infinite, Power and Lightning give every player the same starting hand. Power also removes the 9s and 10s, which breaks the Hi-Lo tags. Lightning charges a fee equal to 100% of the ante ([Studio Spins](https://studiospins.com/blog/how-to-play-live-blackjack-online-evolution-table-families/); [Wizard of Vegas](https://wizardofvegas.com/forum/gambling/blackjack/36643-lightning-blackjack-from-evolution/)). Standard indices do not transfer to any of them.

## Hi-Lo with exact deck counts puts the real entry at true count +3

**Use Hi-Lo** (2–6 = +1, 7–9 = 0, 10–A = −1). True count is the running count divided by the decks remaining ([Wizard of Odds](https://wizardofodds.com/games/blackjack/card-counting/high-low/)). Because a wonger only plays positive counts, the count's betting correlation matters far more than its playing efficiency. Hi-Lo's betting correlation is about 0.97. Higher-level counts gain little: Snyder calls their superiority "negligible", and another estimate puts the gain at about 0.1% ([LVA](https://www.lasvegasadvisor.com/blog/whats-the-best-card-counting-system/)). Software has no mental load, so running Wong Halves alongside Hi-Lo as a cross-check costs nothing. Make Hi-Lo the source of truth, because the published indices are keyed to it. Published simulations estimate decks to the nearest half deck ([Blackjack in Color](https://blackjackincolor.com/truecount5.htm)). The tool can do better and compute **decks remaining = (416 − cards seen) / 52**, keeping the true count as an unrounded float. Use flooring only when looking up an integer index.

Each Hi-Lo true count is worth **about 0.5% of player edge** ([Snyder/Wong via LVA](https://www.lasvegasadvisor.com/blog/an-excerpt-from-arnold-snyders-beat-the-6-deck-game/)). Rule-by-rule arithmetic from Wizard of Odds data gives a slightly better baseline. It starts from 0.66% for 8 decks with the dealer hitting soft 17 and double after split allowed. Standing on soft 17 is worth about +0.19%, and losing double after split costs about 0.12–0.16% ([Wikipedia/Wizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackjack); [Wizard of Odds Appendix 4](https://wizardofodds.com/games/blackjack/appendix/4/)). That puts the baseline near −0.6%. The published RTP implies **−0.71%**, so use that as the conservative baseline. A linear model, EV ≈ −0.71% + 0.5% × TC, gives the table below. The figures are arithmetic, not simulation output, and the real curve is somewhat nonlinear.

| True count | Estimated edge | Full-Kelly bet (variance ≈ 1.3) | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | −0.21% | — | Watch |
| +1.5 | ≈ +0.04% (break-even) | — | Exit threshold |
| +2 | +0.29% | 0.22% of bankroll | Marginal; enter only with a strong peek/original-bets rule |
| +3 | +0.79% | 0.61% | **Default entry**; take insurance |
| +4 | +1.29% | 0.99% | Raise bet |
| +5 | +1.79% | 1.38% | Maximum bet |

These thresholds agree with the only peer-reviewed back-counting study. Werthamer found the optimal 6-deck entry is true count +2 to +3. He also found that an exit threshold improves results "only slightly", and that the risk of ruin is low enough that back-counters can flat bet ([Werthamer 2006, IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/intgms/v6y2006i2p111-122.html)). Practitioner simulations for 8 decks enter at +1 with a 1-2-4-6-12 unit ramp. Those runs used 75–88% penetration and better rules, though, so +1 entries carry over poorly to Evolution's 0.71% baseline ([Casino News Daily](https://www.casinonewsdaily.com/blackjack-guide/evaluating-eight-deck-games/)). **Default to entering at TC ≥ +3, exiting below +1.5 or at the shuffle, and betting half Kelly.** Make the entry threshold a setting, and lower it to +2.5 only if you confirm an Ace-and-10 peek or original-bets-only rule.

For index plays, use Schlesinger's **Illustrious 18**, published verbatim by Wizard of Odds ([Wizard of Odds](https://wizardofodds.com/games/blackjack/card-counting/high-low/)). Store them as a lookup from (hand, dealer upcard) to (index, direction). Insurance at +3 is the most valuable entry, and since you will only play at +3 and above, you will take insurance on almost every dealer Ace. The others that matter in that range are 16 v 10 stand (0), 15 v 10 stand (+4), 10,10 v 6 split (+4) and v 5 (+5), 10 v 10 double (+4), 12 v 3 stand (+2), 12 v 2 stand (+3), 11 v A double (+1), 10 v A double (+4) and 9 v 7 double (+3). Skip the Fab 4 surrender plays, because the game has no surrender. Two adjustments apply. Without a peek on 10s, doubling against a 10 or Ace is worse, so tag those indices for review. And in 6-deck simulations, adding every index on top of the I18 is worth only 0.02–0.035% ([Wizard of Odds](https://wizardofodds.com/games/blackjack/card-counting/high-low/)), so do not build a full index matrix.

**Risk is where the 50% penetration hurts most.** Variance per hand is about 1.3 ([Wizard of Odds Appendix 4](https://wizardofodds.com/games/blackjack/appendix/4/)). Take a flat 1-unit bet at an average edge of 0.8%. N0, the number of hands before results stand one standard deviation above zero, is 1.3 / 0.008², or about **20,000 hands played**. The bankroll needed for 5% risk of ruin, B = σ²·ln(20) / (2μ), is about **245 units**. At an average edge of 0.3% (entering at +2), those become about 144,000 hands and 650 units. These are formula results, not simulations. No published table gives true-count frequencies for 8 decks at 50% penetration. The nearest data shows how much depth matters. In 6-deck games, the win rate with half a deck cut off is seven times the win rate with 2.5 decks cut off ([Blackjack in Color](https://blackjackincolor.com/penetration3.htm)). In 8-deck games, going from 6.5 to 7 decks dealt raises SCORE from 10.27 to 18.49 ([Blackjack Attack via blackjackinfo forum](https://www.blackjackinfo.com/community/threads/what-penetration-increase-is-needed-for-8-deck-to-beat-6.13102/)). Also check the notes' estimate of 30–40 rounds per shoe against your own logs. A full seven-seat table plus the dealer deals eight hands per round, so 208 cards may last only about ten rounds. That leaves very few chances per shoe to reach +3, and it is the main argument for watching several tables at once.

## A slot-based pipeline turns pixels into a trustworthy count

Capture speed is not the bottleneck. On Windows, DXcam reaches about 239 fps against python-mss's 76 fps. mss is cross-platform and thread-safe ([DXcam README](https://github.com/ra1nty/DXcam); [python-mss](https://python-mss.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)). A competitor's benchmark reports stale frames in DXcam's streaming mode ([rustcam](https://gitlab.com/zenham/rustcam/-/tags/v0.0.5)). So poll `grab(region=…)` at 10–15 Hz and do not use `start()`. Cards stay on the table for seconds, so this rate misses nothing.

Card recognition has two paths, and the client's appearance decides between them. If Evolution's client draws digital card icons or hand-total badges (unconfirmed, so check), match those sprites with `cv2.matchTemplate` at a fixed browser zoom. This is cheap and close to exact. The OpenCV forum's warning that template matching "will not work" applies to unconstrained photos of physical cards, not to fixed UI sprites ([OpenCV forum](https://forum.opencv.org/t/using-template-matching-to-identify-playing-cards/3390)). If the client shows only video, fine-tune a small detector. Dubach fine-tuned YOLO11s on 117 online-blackjack screenshots containing 409 cards, later adding 60 more screenshots. His model rose from **80.5% to 99.5% mAP@50** once he fixed label boxes that had captured face-down cards. Training took 13 minutes on a free Colab T4, and inference took about 45 ms per frame locally ([Dubach](https://philippdubach.com/posts/counting-cards-with-computer-vision/index.md)). On CPU, YOLO11n runs at about 56 ms per frame through ONNX, and Ultralytics now points new work to YOLO26 ([Ultralytics](https://docs.ultralytics.com/models/yolo11/)). Train on **rank only, plus a "card back" class**, because suits are irrelevant and mistaking a 10 for a King costs nothing in Hi-Lo. Generic Roboflow datasets of 6,000–24,000 images exist ([Roboflow](https://universe.roboflow.com/augmented-startups/playing-cards-ow27d/dataset/4)). They are photographs of physical cards, though, so a few hundred labeled frames of the real stream are worth more. Do not use OCR for card corners: EasyOCR missed about half of cards that were already cropped ([Dubach](https://philippdubach.com/posts/counting-cards-with-computer-vision/index.md)). Use OCR only for banners and hand totals.

**De-duplication is where hobby projects fail.** AceTrack-AI applies a 4-second cooldown to each card class ([search result](https://gh.r-mj.com/zer0dayf/AceTrack-AI)). That approach drops real cards whenever the same rank appears twice in one round, which happens all the time with 8 decks. Instead, give every card a spatial slot: seat 1–7 × card position, plus the dealer's up card and hole card. Commit a card only after it has held the same rank in the same slot for K consecutive frames above a confidence threshold. Never re-count a committed slot. Use Ultralytics' ByteTrack or BoT-SORT track IDs so that a card moved during a split keeps its identity. Count the hole card only when the card back flips to a face. If a hole card is never revealed, it stays uncounted and the true-count denominator should include it as unseen.

Claude vision serves as a fallback for low-confidence or disputed slots. A 200×200 crop costs 64 visual tokens. The docs warn about images under 200 px, so upscale crops to at least that size. Request output with `output_config.format` and a JSON-schema enum that includes `"unknown"` ([Anthropic Vision](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision); [Structured Outputs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)). Dubach measured 99.9% accuracy on YOLO crops but found it "too slow for real-time" ([Dubach](https://philippdubach.com/posts/counting-cards-with-computer-vision/index.md)). So batch the crops and apply the answers asynchronously as count corrections. No published latency figure exists, so measure it yourself, and check current prices at [claude.com/pricing](https://claude.com/pricing).

| Module | Responsibility | Key design rule |
|---|---|---|
| `capture` | mss (DXcam on Windows) region grab at 10–15 Hz | Store the region as fractions of the window; the browser must not be covered |
| `layout` | One-time calibration of ROIs: 7 seats, dealer, shoe, banner, timer, seat-free indicators | Lock the browser zoom; detect camera-angle changes and pause |
| `detect` | Sprite template match **or** YOLO rank + card-back detector | Rank-only classes; a card-back negative class |
| `track` | Slot state machine plus track IDs | Commit after K stable frames; never re-count |
| `validate` | OCR hand total vs sum of committed cards; expected card count per hand | On mismatch, send the crop to the fallback |
| `llm_fallback` | Async Claude call on upscaled crops | JSON-schema enum with "unknown"; batch requests |
| `round_fsm` | Betting → dealing → player actions → dealer reveal → settle | Boundary = betting timer or banner, plus empty slots |
| `shoe_fsm` | Detects a new shoe and resets RC = 0, cards seen = 0 | Triggers: shuffle banner, shoe swap, cut-card sighting, long gap, **manual hotkey** |
| `count` | Hi-Lo RC, unrounded TC with exact deck resolution, Halves in parallel | Track unresolved cards; mark the shoe **degraded** if any remain |
| `decide` | Entry/exit, half-Kelly bet, I18 lookup, insurance | Alert only if TC ≥ entry **and** a seat is free **and** the shoe is not degraded |
| `alert` | Always-on-top overlay, audio cue, local web dashboard | UI runs on its own thread or process, fed from a queue |
| `log` | SQLite: every committed card with frame reference, confidence, source; shoe and round metadata | This is also your observation dataset |

One missed or misread card moves the running count by 1. With four decks left, that moves the true count by 0.25. So the "degraded shoe" flag matters more than raw recognition accuracy. Suppress alerts whenever unresolved cards could move the true count across the entry threshold.

## Terms of service, not statutes, are the binding constraint

Card counting in your head is lawful in every jurisdiction checked. Devices are a different matter. Nevada's NRS 465.075 bans using "any device to assist… in keeping track of cards played", and regulators applied it to an iPhone counting app in 2009 ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/nevada/2010/title41/chapter465/nrs465-075.html); [NGCB notice](https://gaming.nv.gov/uploadedFiles/gamingnvgov/content/about/industry-notices/2009-02-05-.pdf)). Several states copy that wording, including Mississippi, Colorado, Illinois, Indiana and Iowa, with penalties of up to five years in Iowa ([LVA/Blackjack Forum, 1994](https://www.lasvegasadvisor.com/blog/blackjack-computers-3/)). The statutes say "at a licensed gaming establishment", and **no source shows them being applied to someone playing online from home**. New Jersey, the largest online casino market, makes device use "in a licensed casino or simulcasting facility" a disorderly persons offense. NJ internet gaming is legally conducted through Atlantic City licensees, so a prosecutor could argue that online play counts. That argument has never been tested. In the UK, s.42 of the Gambling Act 2005 covers "deception or interference in connection with the process" of gambling, with up to two years in prison ([legislation.gov.uk](https://legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/19/section/42)). After *Ivey v Genting*, cheating does not require dishonesty ([Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/uk-court-rules-gamblers-advantage-play-constitutes-cheating)), and the Gambling Commission holds no guidance on player-owned devices ([UKGC FOI](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/about-us/freedomofinformation/section-42-cheating)). That makes a software counting aid used against an operator's terms the riskiest version of this conduct in the UK. No prosecution of an online live-blackjack counter was found anywhere.

The near-certain exposure is contractual. A US operator's own guide states that counting is legal but that "using bots, third party software, or breaking site terms" violates the rules ([Hard Rock Bet](https://www.hardrock.bet/casino/how-to-play-blackjack/card-counting/)). US operator agreements typically reserve the right to close accounts "with or without notice" ([LegalBlackjackSites](https://www.legalblackjacksites.com/player-rights/account-closures/)). Vague "irregular play" clauses let operators void winnings, often discovered only at withdrawal ([Casino Sites guide](https://casinosites.bet/guides/terms-that-void-winnings/)). No verbatim clause from bet365, BetMGM, DraftKings, FanDuel, Stake or LeoVegas could be retrieved, so read your own operator's "prohibited software" and "irregular play" sections before you use any real money. Regulated US and UK markets give you a regulator or an ADR body (NJ DGE complaints, IBAS) to appeal to. Offshore balances that are withheld are "frequently gone" ([USABlackjackSites](https://www.usablackjacksites.com/safety/account-closures/)).

Detection is structurally easy for the operator. Spectate-then-join produces a clear signature: you sit down mid-shoe, bet only late in the shoe, rarely play at neutral counts, and your bet size tracks the true count. The operator has every card and every bet in its server logs. The land-based i-Shoe already flags counters by correlating bet size with the count ([Zutis & Hoey, Waterloo](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/research/blackjack/)). No published operator detection model or confiscation ruling for live blackjack was found, and the forum reports of seized funds date from 2013 ([Wizard of Vegas](https://wizardofvegas.com/forum/gambling/online/12798-card-counting-at-online-blackjack/)). Assume you will be detected if you win consistently. Size your exposure as if any balance at the operator could be frozen.

## Your own logs must settle these open questions

Nothing below should be filled in from secondary sources. Each item is an input the tool needs, and the `log` module should record most of them automatically once you start watching. Until then, log them by hand. Collect at least 20–30 complete shoes per table before you trust a penetration figure or the frequency of high true counts.

| # | Observe and log | How | What it configures |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Full in-game Help/Rules text, with screenshot and date, per table | Open the help panel | Rules profile; re-check monthly |
| 2 | Published RTP shown in the help panel | Help panel | Baseline edge, break-even true count |
| 3 | Dealer behaviour with a 10 up: is there a peek, is the hole card dealt face down, what happens to doubles and splits against a blackjack | Watch rounds where a 10 is up and the dealer has blackjack | Peek mode; index adjustments |
| 4 | Is the hole card always revealed, including when every player busts | Watch rounds where every player busts | Unseen-card accounting |
| 5 | **Cards dealt per shoe** (count from the first card after a shuffle to the last before the next one) | Logger or manual tally | Penetration setting; decks-remaining denominator |
| 6 | Cut-card visibility: is it shown, and where it lands | Watch shoe loading | Optional penetration cross-check |
| 7 | **Shuffle and new-shoe signals**: banner text, timer pause, dealer gestures, shoe swap between the two shoes, how long it takes | Screen recordings of 10+ transitions | `shoe_fsm` triggers |
| 8 | Do the two shoes alternate? Is the second shoe pre-shuffled, and is it ever partially used? | Watch several transitions | Count reset logic |
| 9 | Burn cards at the start of a new shoe (number, and whether shown) | Watch shoe start | Initial cards-seen value |
| 10 | Per-round reshuffle or continuous-shuffle behaviour on each table | Note whether shoe transitions happen at all | Exclude the table |
| 11 | **Seat availability**: how often a seat is free, and for how long | Sample the lobby or table every few minutes | Feasibility; multi-table need |
| 12 | Can you take a seat mid-shoe? Do seats open only between rounds, and is there a delay before your first bet? | Try it with a minimum bet | Alert timing |
| 13 | **Seat-timeout rounds**: how many rounds you can skip before losing the seat, and whether a "sit out" option exists | Sit and skip rounds | Exit strategy (leave vs sit out) |
| 14 | Length of the betting window and the decision timer, and what happens by default on timeout | Time it | Alert lead time |
| 15 | Digital card icons and hand totals: present for every seat, or only your own? | Inspect the client | Template matching vs YOLO path |
| 16 | How splits are drawn on screen (layout, card positions) | Watch splits | Slot and track design |
| 17 | Camera angle changes, zooms, replays or overlays covering cards | Recordings | Layout pause rules |
| 18 | **Rounds per hour and cards per round** at full and partial tables | Logger | Opportunities per shoe and per hour |
| 19 | Stream delay compared with the betting timer | Compare events | Whether the count is current at bet time |
| 20 | Table limits, and the minimum-to-maximum bet range | Lobby | Bet ramp and units |
| 21 | Frequency of true count ≥ +2 / +3 / +4 by shoe depth (computed from your logged cards) | `count` module output | Entry threshold; expected value per hour |
| 22 | Recognition errors: unresolved cards per shoe, and how often the fallback was used | `log` module | Degraded-shoe threshold |
| 23 | Your operator's T&C clauses on software, bots and irregular play (exact text and date) | Read the terms | Go/no-go decision |

The schema should store these fields for every committed card: table, shoe ID, round number, slot, rank, confidence, source (template, YOLO, LLM or manual), frame timestamp, and running count after the card. For each shoe, store the start and end signals, cards seen, and the reason the shoe ended. That gives you the 8-deck, 50%-penetration true-count frequency table that no published source provides, built from cards you actually saw.

## Conclusion

The engineering is the easy part. A rank-only detector, slot-based de-duplication and a manual shoe-reset hotkey give you a reliable count within a weekend of labeling. Whether the project makes money depends on three numbers that no one has published: actual penetration, 10-up peek behaviour, and how often a seat is free when the count reaches +3. The sensible order is to run the tool in log-only mode first. Treat it as a scientific instrument and leave the betting assistant for later. Use your own shoe logs to compute the true-count frequency, then decide whether there is an edge.

If measured penetration comes back near 75%, the game becomes ordinary but playable shoe counting. If it comes back near 50%, the arithmetic above implies tens of thousands of hands at a fraction of a percent edge, at a casino that can see every bet next to every card and can void your balance on terms you agreed to. In that case, the most valuable output of the project is the data showing it is not worth betting on.
